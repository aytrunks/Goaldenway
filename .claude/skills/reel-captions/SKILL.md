# Reel captions for trade and labor videos

The footage is usually one simple clip: a painter cutting a line, pulling tape, rolling a wall, sanding.
The footage alone doesn't hold attention. The text does. Write for someone scrolling on their phone
with the sound off, who decides in about 1.5 seconds whether to keep watching.

## Audience

Painters, contractors, handymen, and tradespeople, plus homeowners who like satisfying work.
They respond to:

- **Being seen:** difficult customers, early mornings, sore backs, lowball offers, "while you're here…"
- **Pride in craft:** clean lines, no-tape cuts, years of experience, "this is why we charge what we charge."
- **Satisfaction:** tape pulls, crisp edges, the before and after.
- **Crew humor:** apprentices, bosses, supply prices, paint colors with silly names.

Avoid anything that punches down at customers by name, makes gendered jokes, or insults another trade.
Keep the teasing affectionate.

## Output format 1: Hook captions (one line, first 1–3 seconds)

- 3–10 words. One idea. Readable in under 2 seconds.
- Use one of these hooks: **relatable pain**, **POV or skit setup**, **satisfying promise**, **curiosity gap**,
  **pride or flex**, **comment bait** (rate it, argue it, tag someone).
- The line should make the viewer want to see the next 5 seconds of the clip, and should fit almost any painting shot.
- Group the output by hook type, and give at least 8 per type unless asked otherwise.

## Output format 2: Pop-up text sequences (words appearing throughout the video)

Each sequence is 5–8 **beats**. A beat is a short phrase that pops on screen for about 1.5–2 seconds.
The text doesn't have to describe the footage. Its job is to keep people reading until the end.

Apply these principles:

1. **Open a loop in beat 1** (a question, a countdown, a promise, or an unfinished story) and **close it in the last beat.**
   People keep watching to get closure.
2. **Every beat raises a small question** that the next beat answers. Never let the viewer feel finished early.
3. **Keep beats to 6 words or fewer.** Reading is effort, so make each beat quick.
4. **Numbers and countdowns** (#3, #2, #1 or 5…4…3) make progress visible and keep people watching.
5. **Put a pattern interrupt in the middle** ("wait—", "not yet", "…kidding") to reset attention at the point where people usually drop off.
6. **Speak directly to the viewer:** "you", "watch his hand", "did you catch it?"
7. **Make the ending loop back:** the last beat points back to the start ("watch it again", "now look at the brush"),
   which causes rewatches.
8. **Finish with a comment prompt** when it fits ("rate it 1–10 👇", "tag your painter").

Write each sequence as a numbered list of beats with a working title. Say where the payoff beat should hold longer,
and match beats to on-screen moments where it makes sense (for example, the tape pull lands on the final beat).

## Production notes to include

- Put text in the upper-middle third of the frame, clear of the UI and captions. Use a large bold sans font with an outline or shadow.
- Change beats on visual actions (a brush stroke, a tape pull) when possible.
- Hold the final beat about 1 second longer than the others.
- Keep total length at about 7–15 seconds so the loop feels natural.
