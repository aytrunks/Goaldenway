# Caption Bank

The day-by-day rotation that used to be here is superseded — the real workflow is per-boat, not
per-day: each boat gets all 3 formats (long-form, short-form, carousel) from one edit session,
using one hook across all three. **Use the
[campaign hub](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/31d47080-873c-4742-90af-e4295aeb0c8e)** for live
captions — it generates all 3 from whichever hook is current and copies them straight to your
clipboard. This file is now just the hook-writing reference below.

Template:
```
[HOOK STATEMENT]
The Boat House Cape Coral. 📍 [CTA — rotates: DM me / DM or stop by / DM to book]
```

Formats, corrected to match the real edits:
- **Long-form** — 1:00–1:35, full detail process start to finish
- **Short-form** — 7–18s, quick before/after reveal
- **Carousel** — before/after photo slides

## New Reynolds-style hooks (R6-R13) — written after studying the real ads

The brief's 5 Reynolds-style hooks (R1-R5) are all one move: self-aware roast of the boat, then
pivot. Real Ryan Reynolds/Maximum Effort ads (Mint Mobile, Aviation Gin) run a wider playbook than
that — so these eight are each built on a *different* technique pulled from actual campaigns, not
just more one-liners in the same shape. Use the technique notes to write your own once you see
which ones land.

| ID | Hook | Technique (from the real ads) |
|---|---|---|
| **R6** | "This is technically an ad. Your boat is technically a health hazard. Let's fix the more urgent one." | **Meta / fourth-wall.** Mint Mobile ads openly admit they're ads ("I know this is an ad") instead of pretending otherwise — it disarms the viewer's skepticism before the pitch even starts. |
| **R7** | "I clean boats for a living and still can't parallel park mine. Nobody's perfect. Yours can be, though." | **Self-deprecation redirected.** Reynolds' jokes land on *himself*, never the customer — then the joke pivots the stakes onto the product. |
| **R8** | "We don't do 'ceramic-infused hydrophobic ecosystems.' We do clean boats. Revolutionary, we know." | **Roast the jargon.** Mint's whole angle is mocking big carriers' confusing pricing language. Same move here: mock the industry's overcomplicated marketing-speak, then flex how simple you actually are. |
| **R9** | "There's a version of your boat that looks incredible in golden hour light. It's currently buried under three months of neglect." | **Sincere setup, comedic/blunt turn.** Straight from the Aviation Gin "Hot Frosty" spoof structure — play it completely straight for a beat, then undercut it hard. Also doubles as the brand's own golden-hour story. |
| **R10** | "Your hull has more baggage than my last relationship, and that one ended over a group chat." | **Absurd personal comparison.** Non-sequitur humor — the comparison doesn't have to logically track, it just has to land a laugh before snapping back to the boat. |
| **R11** | "Neglected boats lose value fast. Statistic. I should mention I have zero peer-reviewed studies. The boat thing's still true." | **Mock-authoritative, then honest.** Reynolds states a "fact" with total confidence, then immediately admits he made it up — the honesty about the bit is funnier than the bit, and the underlying claim stays true. |
| **R12** | "Your neighbors aren't talking about your boat behind your back. They're being polite about it to your face. Which is worse." | **Escalating social guilt.** Builds on the brief's "your boat's judging you" thread but moves the discomfort from the boat itself to other people noticing — a different pressure point. |
| **R13** | "DM us, or don't, and let the boat keep doing that. Your call. We'll wait. It will not wait well." | **Deadpan, low-pressure close.** Reynolds' CTAs rarely beg — they shrug, which reads as more confident than a hard sell, right up until the last clause reintroduces the stakes. |
