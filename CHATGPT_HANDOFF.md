Hi ChatGPT. I'm handing you a project already in progress. Your job: **help me write the best possible prompts for AI video tools** (Wan 2.2, Kling, Runway, Veo, Sora or similar) to make short social videos featuring **real products from our pawn shop**. Please read all of this before writing anything.

---

## 2. The goal
Get people **walking in the door** to **buy** or **sell**. Every video should leave the viewer thinking either "I need to go see that" or "they'd pay me for my stuff?"

- **Music:** acoustic and electric guitars with cases, microphones, speakers (small/medium/big), keyboards
- **Gaming:** PS5, PS4, PS4 Pro, Xbox One / One S, Nintendo Switch, controllers, games
- **Tools:** DeWalt, Milwaukee, Ryobi power tools and batteries, blowers, cutters
- **Jewelry:** rings, chains, necklaces, bracelets, gold and silver

## 4. Brand look and voice
- **Colours (sampled from the website):** sun yellow `#FFD42A`, soft yellow `#FDDF67`, near-black `#1C1C1C`, black `#000000`, pale green `#EFF4D2`
- **Mood:** warm Florida sunshine, golden hour, friendly, local. The website hero is a sunset over a Cape Coral canal with palm trees.
- **Voice:** fun, warm, a little funny, never salesy. Lowercase, casual captions. No "BUY NOW" or "SALE."
- **Sign-off:** "Stay Sunny"

## 5. Content already planned (prompts should support these)
1. **"Sunny Pawn Adoption Center":** parody of sad animal-shelter commercials. Items get names and sad backstories ("This is Dewey the DeWalt drill. He hasn't drilled a hole in 43 days."). Black & white and slow-mo, then a hard cut to bright colour and "Adopt today at Sunny Pawn & Jewelry."
2. **Guitar song reel:** an AI song (made in Suno) sung from the point of view of a lonely guitar waiting for a home. Considered styles: heartfelt acoustic, upbeat folk-pop, 1950s rockabilly (Elvis-style).
3. **"Real or Fake?":** rapid-fire jewelry with "REAL ✅" stamps. Twist ending: "ALL REAL. we test everything."
4. **Console drop:** PS5 opens the video, then PS4s, Switch, controllers; "wait for the last one," and the Xbox reveal at the end.
5. **"This camera/drone filmed this video":** product demos where the item made the footage.

## 6. Where AI video fits
AI clips are for **intros, transitions and dramatic moments**, not for replacing real product footage. Examples:
- A guitar sitting alone in a dusty, golden-hour shop corner (Adoption Center intro)
- A PS5 slowly rotating on a glowing yellow surface
- Jewelry sparkling as sunlight sweeps across it
- The hero sunset with palm trees gently moving, as a brand intro/outro
- A drill "waking up" in a dramatic slow push-in

**Best method:** **image-to-video**. I photograph the REAL item in the shop, and the AI animates that photo (camera move, lighting, small motion). That keeps the product accurate.

## 7. Rules (important)
1. **Never misrepresent a product.** The AI must not change the item's brand, model, colour, condition or accessories. Use the real photo as the first frame and keep motion to camera moves and lighting.
2. **No guns** in any video for the main account (platform rules). The shop is an FFL dealer, but firearms stay out of this content.
3. **No real people's faces or customers.** No celebrity likenesses or voices (we describe a style instead of naming an artist).
4. **No other brands' logos as the focus** (e.g., don't build a video around Hublot/Bulgari logos).
5. **Platforms may require an "AI-generated" label** on realistic AI content (Instagram/Facebook/TikTok). Remind me when it applies.
6. **Real prices only.** Prices are added later as text in CapCut. Never put prices or text inside the AI prompt (AI text comes out garbled).

## 8. Technical specs
- **Format:** vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) for Reels/TikTok
- **Clip length:** 5–10 seconds per AI clip (stitched together in CapCut)
- **Editing:** CapCut, where I add text, prices, sound effects (ding, whoosh, cha-ching) and music

## 9. What I need from you
For each video idea, give me:
1. **A main prompt** in this structure: *subject (exact item as in my photo) → action/motion → camera move → lighting → mood/style → setting*
2. **A negative prompt** (what to avoid: warped hands, changed logos, extra parts, text, distortion, flicker)
3. **Settings tips** (clip length, motion strength, image-to-video vs text-to-video)
4. **2 variations** (one safer and realistic, one more cinematic)
5. **How the clip fits the edit**, e.g. "use as the first 2 seconds before the hard cut"

Keep prompts **specific and visual** (lens, light direction, camera speed), not vague ("cool," "amazing").
